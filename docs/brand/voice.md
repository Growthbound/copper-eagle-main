# Copper Eagle brand voice

**Status: drafted from the live site, not yet confirmed by the owner.** This
guide describes how Copper Eagle Plumbing already writes on
coppereagleplumbing.com (the Squarespace site this one replaces), so new copy
sounds like the rest of the site. When the owner reviews it, record their
changes here and drop this status line.

This file is binding on every word a customer reads: website and service pages,
city pages, blog articles, FAQs, emails, texts, ads, calls to action, page
titles, and meta descriptions. It is silent on code, comments, commit messages,
and internal documentation.

## Core brand personality

A local, owner-run plumbing company in Salt Lake City. Jordan Walker is the
owner and a master plumber, and the site speaks for him and a small, named team
(Matt, Dalton, Holden, Spencer, JD, and Matthew "Hibs" in the office). The
company talks like a neighbor who happens to be a plumber: friendly, confident,
and straightforward about prices and timing.

It writes in the first person plural ("we", "our team") to one reader ("you",
"your home"), and it is proud of being local: Salt Lake City, Park City, and
"local Utah areas" come up often.

## Primary voice principles

- **Reassuring first.** Pages open by taking the worry off the reader's plate:
  "Life's too short to worry about your home's plumbing." "Has disaster struck
  in the middle of the night?" Lead with the reader's problem, then say how
  Copper Eagle handles it.
- **Plain promises about how the job goes**, not about how great the company
  is. "We show you pricing before we start. No surprises here!" "If our Pro is
  running late he will let you know in advance." Say what happens.
- **Warm and a little informal.** Contractions, the odd exclamation mark, and
  a friendly sign-off ("Let us help..."). Never stiff, never jokey.
- **Confident, not hyped.** The house style is "Done right, every time." Short,
  sure statements beat superlatives.
- **Local and specific.** Name the place (Salt Lake City, Draper, Park City)
  and the thing (water heater, main water line, steam shower), not "your area"
  and "your system".

## Patterns the site uses

- **Eyebrows** above headings are short, uppercase, and often locate the
  reader: "SALT LAKE CITY + SURROUNDING AREAS", "AFTER HOURS PLUMBING",
  "DONE RIGHT, EVERY TIME".
- **Headings** are either a reassurance ("The ultimate peace of mind.") or a
  plain service name ("Drain Cleaning and Repair Services"). Service names use
  "+" in short labels ("Drain Cleaning + Repair").
- **Calls to action** are direct and usually uppercase on buttons: "GET IN
  TOUCH WITH US", "CONTACT US TODAY", "CALL OR TEXT", plus the phone number.
  The company answers calls and texts, so offer both.
- **Closing bands** invite the next step in one sentence: "Let our friendly and
  competent team of experienced plumbers help you today!"
- **Explanatory copy** on service pages teaches: what the problem is, the
  warning signs, and when to call. Keep that helpful, reader-first structure.

## Writing new copy (city pages, FAQs, blog posts)

- Put the useful answer first, in a sentence or two.
- Prefer facts the reader can check (where the water comes from, how hard it
  is, how old the homes are) over adjectives. Name the source for local facts.
- Use short sentences and everyday words. "Pipes", not "piping
  infrastructure".
- Match the site's warmth, but don't copy its typos or its biggest claims into
  new places.

## Words and phrases to avoid

- Hype and superlatives in new copy ("best in Utah", "world-class",
  "unbeatable"). The live site uses some; they stay where the owner wrote them
  until the owner decides, but new copy doesn't add more.
- Fear-based selling and fake urgency ("act now", "before it's too late",
  "limited time").
- Filler ("elevate", "comprehensive solutions", "tailored to your unique
  needs").

## Accuracy and integrity

Never publish a claim the owner has not confirmed: founding year, licenses,
certifications, insurance, awards, reviews or ratings, prices, guarantees,
service areas, response times, staff names, or project results. The company's
own published wording (the live site and its export) counts as the owner's
words; new copy may restate it but must not stretch it. Missing evidence blocks
the claim. It is not permission for a placeholder.

Local facts about a city (water supplier and hardness, housing age, elevation,
neighborhoods, permit office) come from a public source that is cited on the
page.

Legal and regulated pages (privacy policy, terms and conditions, text-message
consent, disclaimers) come from the owner or a legal review and are never
rewritten as marketing copy.

## Review checklist

Before any customer-facing copy ships:

- [ ] Every claim about the company comes from the owner or their published copy.
- [ ] Every local fact has a cited public source.
- [ ] Nothing is borrowed from another brand built on this platform.
- [ ] The useful answer comes first.
- [ ] It sounds like the live site: friendly, local, confident, plain.
- [ ] No new hype, fear, or fake urgency.
- [ ] Phone, email, and address come from site settings, not the copy.
- [ ] Legal and consent text is untouched unless a rewrite was explicitly authorized.
